## Title:Environmental gradients shift basal energy pathways while preserving trophic architecture in oyster reef food webs

**Abstract:** Environmental gradients can structure food webs by altering energy pathways and trophic interactions, yet these effects across broad spatial scales remain poorly understood. We examined how environmental conditions and habitat structure influence basal resource use, trophic level, and trophic niche structure of oyster reef communities across four estuaries spanning the South Atlantic Bight (SAB). This biogeographic region exhibits substantial yet predictable variation in tidal amplitude, which strongly infleunces oyster reef structure. Across the SAB, consumer resource use varied with reef characteristics, including reef slope and percent cover highly correlated with tidal amplitude. In low-tidal amplitude systems with shallow reef slopes such as North Carolina and northeast Florida, suspended particulate organic matter (sPOM) fueled consumer production, whereas high-tidal amplitude systems with steep reef slopes, such as South Carolina and Georgia, were primarily supported by benthic microalgae. Despite these shifts, dominant energy pathways remained consistent, with basal resources propagating from oysters to higher trophic levels. However, trophic levels were lower in North Carolina and more compressed suggesting shorter food chains and greater transfer efficiency. These results demonstrate that large-scale biophysical gradients regulate both basal energy sources and food chain length while preserving the overall trophic architecture of food webs.
